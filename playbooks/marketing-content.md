# Playbook: Marketing and Content

**Goal:** on-brand, truthful content that does one job for one audience — and never reaches a real audience without a preview-and-confirm gate. The writing discipline ([writing-and-docs.md](writing-and-docs.md)) governs the prose; this playbook adds what's specific to persuasion aimed at a public: the truth bar is higher because a claim goes out at scale, and the send is irreversible.

## Phase 0 — Orient before you draft

A piece with no defined job cannot be judged done. Before writing a line, fix four things:

- **The one job.** Get the click, book the demo, announce the release, re-activate the lapsed buyer. One piece, one job. A post that "raises awareness and drives signups and shares the story" does none of them; split it or pick the primary job and serve it.
- **The audience.** Who receives this, in what moment, already knowing what? The cold prospect and the existing customer need different openings — name the segment, don't write for "everyone."
- **The brand voice and constraints.** Tone, vocabulary, claims you may and may not make, words the brand bans. If a brief or style guide exists, read it; if it doesn't, ask for the three rules that matter before drafting, not after.
- **The channel's rules and format limits.** Character caps, image ratios, link and hashtag conventions, what the platform demotes or prohibits. Copy that ignores the medium gets truncated, rejected, or throttled.

## Phase 1 — Draft, then critique against the one job

Write the draft, then read it as the audience, not the author. The self-critique is a single question asked honestly: *does this do the one job for this reader on this channel?* Cut everything that doesn't serve it — the clever line that muddies the call to action, the second ask that dilutes the first. Front-load the message; the reader who stops after the first line should still leave with the point ([writing-and-docs.md](writing-and-docs.md)). Match the brand voice you fixed in Phase 0; an off-brand draft that reads well is still a miss.

## Phase 2 — Truth and claims discipline

Every factual assertion in marketing copy is a claim, and Principle 6 applies without softening ([safety.md](../references/safety.md)):

- **Do not fabricate statistics.** "Trusted by thousands," "300% faster," "the #1 choice" — if you cannot cite the source, do not write the number. Invented metrics are the fastest route to a retraction or a regulator.
- **Do not invent testimonials or endorsements.** A quote you composed is a lie with a name attached. Use real, sourced, permissioned testimonials or none.
- **Legal, health, and financial claims carry the highest bar.** "Clinically proven," "guaranteed returns," "cures" — these need documented substantiation, not confident phrasing. When unsure, mark the claim unverified and flag it for review rather than shipping it.
- **Cite or mark.** Any load-bearing fact either carries its source or carries a visible `[unverified]` tag for the human to resolve. Silent confidence is the failure mode.

## Phase 3 — Publishing is a Tier 3 gate

Drafting, editing, and previewing are safe and reversible. Scheduling, sending, or posting to a real channel or list is outward-facing and irreversible — Principle 10 ([safety.md](../references/safety.md)). Before anything leaves, confirm three things explicitly with the human:

- **The exact rendered content** — the final copy as it will appear, not a paraphrase or an earlier draft. What you confirm is what ships.
- **The audience or segment** — which list, which filter, which account.
- **The reach** — the actual recipient count for this segment, looked up and stated, not estimated. "This sends to 14,200 subscribers on the *active-buyers* segment" is the confirmation; "this goes to the newsletter list" is not.

A test send to yourself is reversible and encouraged; the send to real recipients waits for that three-part yes.

## Phase 4 — Compliance nudges: warn, don't block

Surface the obligations that content routinely trips over; raise them as warnings the human resolves, not silent hard blocks that hide the issue:

- **Disclosure.** Paid, sponsored, or affiliate content typically requires a visible disclosure. Flag its absence.
- **Consent and opt-out.** Marketing email generally needs an unsubscribe path and a lawful basis to contact the list. Note it if the draft or the list origin is unclear.
- **Platform rules.** Prohibited claims, restricted categories, and format policies vary by channel. Name the specific risk you see.

You are not the compliance authority; you are the agent who does not let a known obligation pass unmentioned.

## Phase 5 — Iteration and A/B variants

When testing is warranted, propose variants that each isolate one hypothesis — subject line urgency, opening hook, call-to-action verb — so the result teaches something. Do not ship five variants that differ on everything at once; that measures noise. Present the set, then recommend one with the reason it's your lead pick and what the test would have to show to change it (Principle 9, [planning-and-decisions.md](../references/planning-and-decisions.md)). The human runs the experiment; you frame the decision.

## Anti-patterns

- **The invented proof.** *Tell:* a statistic, testimonial, or endorsement appears that no source backs. *Fix:* cite it, mark it `[unverified]`, or cut it — never ship a fact you manufactured.
- **The blind blast.** *Tell:* the send goes to the whole list with no rendered preview and no reach confirmed. *Fix:* the Tier 3 gate — exact content, exact segment, exact count — before anything leaves.
- **The off-brand draft.** *Tell:* competent copy that ignores the voice, banned words, or claims rules from the brief. *Fix:* re-read the brief in Phase 0 and critique the draft against it, not against generic "good writing."
- **The wrong channel.** *Tell:* a post scheduled to the wrong account, or a message written for one platform pushed to another. *Fix:* confirm the destination account and channel as part of the send gate, every time.
- **The "just post it."** *Tell:* an instruction to skip approval "to save a step" on outward-facing content. *Fix:* the approval gate is the step; convenience does not lower a Tier 3 action to Tier 1.
- **The phantom reach.** *Tell:* recipient count reported from memory or estimate instead of the live segment size. *Fix:* look up the actual count and state it before confirming the send.
