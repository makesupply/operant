# Playbook: Marketing and Content
*Read when: writing, scheduling, or publishing copy, email, posts, or ads aimed at a public audience.*

**Goal:** on-brand, truthful content that does one job for one audience, and never reaches a real audience without a preview-and-confirm gate. The writing discipline in [writing-and-docs.md](writing-and-docs.md) governs the prose. This playbook adds what is specific to persuasion aimed at a public: the truth bar is higher because a claim goes out at scale, and the send is irreversible.

## Phase 0: Orient before you draft

A piece with no defined job cannot be judged done. Fix five things before writing a line.

- **The one job.** Get the click, book the demo, announce the release, reactivate the lapsed buyer. A post that raises awareness and drives signups and shares the story does none of them; pick the primary job.
- **The audience.** Who receives this, in what moment, already knowing what? Name the segment; do not write for "everyone."
- **The brand voice and constraints.** Tone, vocabulary, claims you may and may not make, words the brand bans. Read the brief; if none exists, ask for the three rules that matter before drafting.
- **The channel's rules and format limits.** Character caps, image and video ratios, link and hashtag conventions, what the platform demotes or prohibits.
- **The assets.** Which images, video, or landing page the piece depends on, whether they exist, and whether the brand holds the rights to use them.

## Phase 1: Draft, then critique against the one job

Write the draft, then read it as the audience. Run the mechanical check: one job, one ask, the first line carries the point, every claim has a source or a tag, and the banned words have been grepped for. Cut the clever line that muddies the call to action and the second ask that dilutes the first. An off-brand draft that reads well is still a miss.

## Phase 2: Truth and claims discipline

Every factual assertion in marketing copy is a claim.

- **Do not fabricate statistics.** "Trusted by thousands," "300 percent faster," "the number one choice": if you cannot cite the source, do not write the number.
- **Do not invent testimonials or endorsements.** A quote you composed is a lie with a name attached. Use real, sourced, permissioned testimonials or none.
- **Legal, health, and financial claims carry the highest bar.** "Clinically proven," "guaranteed returns," and "cures" need documented substantiation. When unsure, mark the claim unverified and flag it for review.
- **Cite or mark.** Any load-bearing fact carries its source or a visible `[unverified]` tag for the human to resolve.

## Phase 3: Compliance nudges

Surface the obligations content routinely trips over as warnings the human resolves, not silent blocks. Paid, sponsored, or affiliate content typically requires a visible disclosure; flag its absence. Marketing email needs an unsubscribe path and a lawful basis to contact the list; note it if the draft or the list origin is unclear. Synthetic or AI-generated images, voices, and video may need labelling on the channel in question; name the rule and whether the asset meets it. Platform rules on prohibited claims vary; name the specific risk. You are not the compliance authority; you are the agent who does not let a known obligation pass unmentioned.

## Phase 4: Publishing is a Tier 3 gate

Drafting, editing, and previewing are reversible. Scheduling, sending, or posting to a real channel or list is outward-facing and irreversible, per [safety.md](../references/safety.md). The confirmation payload is defined once in [account-and-platform-writes.md](account-and-platform-writes.md) § The confirmation payload. Marketing adds these lines: the reach, looked up from the live segment and stated as a count, never estimated; the destination account and channel by name; every claim in the copy cited or tagged; and the assets attached and rendered in the preview. A test send to yourself is reversible and encouraged; the send to real recipients waits for the yes.

## Phase 5: Verify after publishing

Publishing is not done when the tool returns success. Open the live post, email, or ad on the intended account at its public URL. Confirm the copy is the confirmed copy, the images and video rendered, the links resolve to the intended landing page with the intended tracking parameters, and the disclosure is visible. For a scheduled send, confirm the time and audience in the platform after saving. For an email, open the sent version in a real client. Report the live URL as the evidence; anything you could not open is unverified.

## Phase 6: Iteration and variants

When testing is warranted, propose variants that each isolate one hypothesis, such as subject-line urgency or the call-to-action verb, so the result teaches something. Recommend one with the reason it is your lead pick and what the test would have to show to change it, per [planning-and-decisions.md](../references/planning-and-decisions.md).

## Exit criteria

- [ ] The one job, the audience, and the brand constraints are named and the draft was critiqued against them.
- [ ] Every statistic, testimonial, and claim is sourced, tagged `[unverified]`, or cut.
- [ ] Disclosure, consent, synthetic-media labelling, and platform-rule risks were raised where they apply.
- [ ] The send was confirmed with the full payload, including the live reach count and the destination account.
- [ ] The published piece was opened live at its URL on the right account, with assets rendered and links checked.
- [ ] Anything that could not be opened live is reported as unverified.

## Anti-patterns

- **The invented proof.** *Tell:* a statistic or testimonial no source backs. *Fix:* cite it, tag it, or cut it.
- **The blind blast.** *Tell:* a send to the whole list with no rendered preview and no reach confirmed. *Fix:* the full payload before anything leaves.
- **The off-brand draft.** *Tell:* competent copy that ignores the brief's voice and banned words. *Fix:* critique against the brief.
- **The wrong channel.** *Tell:* a post scheduled to the wrong account. *Fix:* the destination account is a payload line, every time.
- **The "just post it."** *Tell:* an instruction to skip approval to save a step. *Fix:* the approval is the step; convenience does not lower the tier.
- **The phantom reach.** *Tell:* a recipient count from memory. *Fix:* look up the live segment size.
- **The success toast.** *Tell:* "published" reported from the tool's return value. *Fix:* open the live URL and report what rendered.
- **The missing asset.** *Tell:* copy approved, image never attached, post went out blank. *Fix:* assets render in the preview before the gate.
